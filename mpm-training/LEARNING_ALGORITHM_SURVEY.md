# Learning algorithm survey for MPM morphogenesis

Research date: 2026-09-07. Scope: the current Python/WebGPU trainer, its existing investigations, primary research papers, and official implementation documentation. This is a literature and implementation survey; no new training experiments were run. Suggested hyperparameters below are experimental starting points, not established optimal settings.

**Recommendation: improve both the search distribution and the survival of alternative developmental behaviors.** First establish reliable validation and benchmark separable CMA-ES against the existing mutation ladder. Then test a small MAP-Elites archive and combine it with limited-memory adaptive search if each component earns its cost. Add NCA-style state pooling as a separate experiment in persistence and repair, after implementing complete simulator-state restoration and continuation beyond capacity stops.

CMA-ES, diversity archives, and NCA pooling operate at different levels:

| Mechanism | What it changes | Main question it addresses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adaptive evolution strategy | How new weight vectors are proposed | Which directions and step sizes improve this policy? |
| Restarts / islands / quality diversity | Which lineages survive and receive search effort | How do we avoid losing every alternative to the current pattern? |
| NCA state pooling / developmental curriculum | Which simulation states and trajectories receive training | Can the policy complete, maintain, and repair shapes from varied states? |
| Repeated evaluation / validation | How evidence of improvement is assessed | Is the apparent improvement repeatable? |

**What the current implementation implies.** The default controller has 6,158 parameters: 33 inputs, 128 tanh hidden units, and 14 outputs. The recurrent 128-unit variant has 9,246 parameters. A generation evaluates 16 policies, keeps the best three, and fills 13 slots with Gaussian mutations of uniformly selected elites. Mutation affects every parameter, with fixed semantic scales and base sigma 0.05. There is no learned covariance, restart rule, lineage protection, behavioral archive, or local gradient refinement. Independent Xavier initializations supply initial diversity; `--initial-weights` instead supplies one parent and its mutants. [Implementation](trainer/evolve.py), [parameter layout](trainer/policy_parameters.py), [initialization](trainer/update_rule.py), [defaults](core/config.json).

The practical diagnosis is broader than a proven mathematical local minimum:

- **Lineages can collapse.** Three elites can all produce essentially the same shape. A child that begins a useful alternative but initially has worse aggregate loss is discarded. Parent selection has no mechanism to preserve such a stepping stone.
- **Mutation scale can miss the useful range.** The default stateless model has root-mean-square mutation-vector length about 3.45 in raw weight space, calculated as `0.05 * sqrt(sum(bucket_scale²))`. That is not a measure of behavioral displacement, but it illustrates why a seemingly small elementwise sigma need not produce a small network change. The optional mutation ladder already permits much finer steps; it does not learn directions or protect diversity.
- **Ranking noise can obscure progress.** Before the latest determinism fixes, the recurrent lizard parent scored 0.8472–0.8883 across four identical-input executions. That historical range is not an estimate of current noise. The subsequent audit fixed allocation/reset issues, but production physics and chemistry still use parallel floating-point reductions. Re-measure current full-horizon repeatability before choosing refinement scales. [Lizard investigation](LIZARD_DETAIL_INVESTIGATION.md), [determinism audit](DETERMINISM_INVESTIGATION.md).
- **Different seed numbers currently need not mean different cases.** With `initialCondition: none`, the initial mesh and state are independent of rollout seed. Repeated seeds can average execution noise, but do not establish robustness to varied starting conditions. Actual perturbation presets must be specified for that purpose. [Reset and seed construction](trainer/training_sim.py), [perturbations](trainer/initial_conditions.py).
- **The stopping rule can hide unstable growth.** Reaching capacity ends and scores the rollout immediately. The five late-window snapshots are therefore bypassed when capacity is reached early. In the historical lizard probes, all 50 full-horizon requests stopped at capacity. A policy can optimize a capacity-limited snapshot without learning to hold a completed shape.
- **The best-ever record can be optimistic.** Both training entry points retain a checkpoint whenever a generation's score beats the historical minimum, without reevaluating the incumbent on the new cases. Elites are reevaluated during selection, but the best-ever record is a separate mechanism. Changing cases and execution noise make those historical minima imperfectly comparable. [CLI](trainer/evolve.py), [server](trainer/train_server.py).

The geometry oracle shows the representation can encode much finer silhouettes than the learned lizard. It does not show that the current controller and physical dynamics can develop them from the seed. Optimizer experiments should therefore test the hypothesis of search failure without assuming reachability has already been established.

**Technique comparison.** Priorities are project-specific judgments, not a universal ranking of algorithms.

| Technique | Expected benefit here | Principal limitation | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mutation ladder + restart / immigrants | Cheap control for step-size mismatch and lineage collapse | Still does not learn correlated directions | Immediate baseline |
| Separable CMA-ES | Adapts coordinate scales, mean, and global step size | Ignores cross-coordinate correlations; can still converge prematurely | First replacement |
| LM-MA-ES / LM-CMA | Learns a compact set of correlated search directions | Limited approximation; local search still needs global exploration | Strong second candidate |
| Full CMA-ES | Rich covariance adaptation | Quadratic state and expensive dense operations at 6k–9k weights | Reduced-space experiment |
| MAP-Elites | Retains distinct shapes that scalar elitism discards | Descriptor design and exploration consume budget | First diversity experiment |
| CMA-ME / scalable CMA-MAE | Combines diversity archives and adaptive emitters | More optimizer state and moving parts | Preferred longer-term combination |
| Novelty + quality / islands | Alternative ways to sustain exploration | Novelty may reward irrelevant variation; migration can erase diversity | Useful controls |
| OpenAI-ES / antithetic ES | Combines information from many perturbations into a direction | Few perturbations give noisy high-dimensional estimates | Secondary baseline |
| Sensitivity-aware / block mutations | Makes refinement less destructive | Local output sensitivity does not capture long-term dynamics | Promising refinement add-on |
| NCA state pooling | Trains completion, persistence, and recovery | Needs complete state replay; does not itself preserve weight-space diversity | Separate training experiment |
| Curriculum / case-based selection | Prevents easy regions or colors dominating all progress | Changes the objective; can preserve unhelpful specialists | Targeted follow-up |
| Differentiable simulation + gradients | Supplies direct parameter credit assignment | Substantial simulator and loss redesign | Longer-term research |

**CMA-ES: learn where and how far to move.** CMA-ES samples around a mean, ranks candidates, recombines successful steps, and adapts a global step size and covariance. Covariance represents directions in which parameters should move together. This is useful when several weights must change coherently to preserve one behavior while improving another. It remains a stochastic search with no guarantee of escaping every basin. Restarts are a separate mechanism. [Hansen, The CMA Evolution Strategy: A Tutorial](https://arxiv.org/html/1604.00772v2).

Full covariance is a material implementation choice here. A single dense float64 matrix requires about **303 MB at 6,158 parameters**, or **684 MB at 9,246 parameters**, calculated as `8 * n²` bytes. Factorizations, additional matrices, workspaces, and multiple emitters add overhead. This is not automatically prohibitive on the current machine, but full CMA should earn that overhead through measured improvement. Dense covariance learning also needs sufficient evaluations; 50 small generations are a limited experiment.

Separable CMA-ES keeps only coordinatewise covariance, while limited-memory methods retain a compact directional approximation. The latter can capture some correlations without a full dense matrix. LM-MA-ES is distinct from LM-CMA; compare named implementations rather than treating them as interchangeable. [Official CMA implementations and diagonal variants](https://cma-es.github.io/cmaes_sourcecode_page.html), [Loshchilov et al., LM-MA-ES](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.06693), [Loshchilov, LM-CMA](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.00221).

My proposed first trial uses one separable optimizer, batch size 32, and the existing semantic scaling as an explicit coordinate transform: `weights = reference_weights + D * search_coordinates`. Start its mean at zero for a warm start, where `D` is the fixed bucket-scale vector. For from-scratch trials, initialize the reference network using the existing initializer. Calibrate initial sigma separately for scratch and refinement; carrying over 0.05 blindly would reproduce a known concern. Avoid applying bucket scaling twice through both the wrapper and library options.

Use the library's normal rank and adaptation rules. Do not splice our three preserved elites into a CMA-generated batch and feed that modified batch back as if CMA had sampled it. Keep a separately validated champion. Restart on library convergence or a predeclared plateau, using either a fresh initialization or a distinct archived policy. Official implementations provide restart machinery, including increasing-population variants; any larger restart must count against the evaluation budget. [CMA implementation guidance](https://cma-es.github.io/cmaes_sourcecode_page.html).

Full CMA is more attractive for a later reduced-space experiment: a low-rank weight perturbation, a chosen block of network parameters, or a small vector of modulation coefficients. Those restrictions can improve search efficiency but also remove necessary solutions. Use them as refinement comparisons, with occasional full-network exploration.

**MAP-Elites: keep alternative shapes alive.** MAP-Elites divides a behavior space into niches and keeps the best policy in each niche. A candidate can survive by improving its niche even when it loses to the global champion. That directly addresses the current tendency to discard a promising new developmental pattern before it becomes globally competitive. This is the strongest conceptual match to the reported “same pattern for many generations” symptom. [Mouret and Clune, Illuminating search spaces by mapping elites](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.04909).

For this simulator, I propose a small initial archive, such as 16 × 16 bins, using two normalized morphology descriptors: principal-axis elongation and a coarse measure of concavity/branching, such as convex-hull deficit. Compute them from coverage, not particle count, because refinement density should not masquerade as a new body plan. Exclude empty/invalid states and assess descriptor noise on repeated rollouts before enabling selection. Distinct limbs can share those descriptors; if that happens, compare against clustering of aligned low-resolution silhouettes. Neither descriptor set should be assumed sufficient in advance.

Make descriptors consistent with the fitness's pose invariance. Otherwise rotated copies of the same pattern can fill the archive. Avoid descriptors consisting only of total loss components: those distinguish failure amounts more readily than developmental strategies. Later, add trajectory descriptors if identical final silhouettes conceal useful differences in growth order. That would require intermediate captures beyond the current terminal result.

The archive must retain policy weights, objective estimates, descriptor estimates, and evaluation context. Rescore provisional improvements and incumbents under comparable cases before treating tiny gains as archive records. Preserve the global champion separately. Archive coverage is a diagnostic; the actual success criterion remains validated target fidelity per unit compute. A larger map full of poor shapes is not success.

**CMA-ME and CMA-MAE combine adaptive search with an archive.** CMA-ME uses CMA-based emitters to propose candidates and pursue archive improvements. CMA-MAE adapts archive acceptance thresholds rather than relying only on immediate replacement of the best occupant, changing how exploration and improvement are balanced. [Fontaine et al., CMA-ME](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.02400), [Fontaine and Nikolaidis, CMA-MAE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.10752).

There is a particularly relevant precedent: Earle et al. evolved collections of NCA level generators using CMA-ME for maze, Sokoban, and Zelda tasks. This establishes that NCA policies can be optimized through quality diversity without differentiating through their rollout. It does not establish performance for continuous MPM growth, long physics trajectories, or our loss. [Illuminating Diverse Neural Cellular Automata for Level Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05489).

For our parameter count, prefer separable or limited-memory emitters if the simpler archive proves useful. Official pyribs documentation supports these scalable variants, including `sep_cma_es` and `lm_ma_es`, and supplies an ask/tell scheduler. This makes the hybrid a practical integration candidate rather than a requirement to implement dense CMA from scratch. [Scalable CMA-MAE documentation](https://docs.pyribs.org/en/latest/tutorials/scalable_cma_mae.html).

A proposed pilot uses two emitters with 16 candidates each, sharing the same 32-candidate batch budget as the single-optimizer comparison. Give them different starting policies or scales and restart stagnant emitters from different archive occupants. This is a hypothesis about useful resource allocation; each emitter sees fewer samples than a single optimizer. Compare both arrangements before adding more emitters. If using pyribs, convert our minimizing loss to a maximizing objective explicitly and configure threshold bounds for that objective; do not copy positive-reward example thresholds unchanged.

**Novelty search and islands are useful alternatives.** Novelty search rewards behavioral distance from previous discoveries. Combining novelty and quality can explore routes that a pure fitness objective ignores; such combinations have been studied with evolution strategies on deceptive reinforcement-learning tasks. [Conti et al., Improving Exploration in Evolution Strategies](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.06560).

My preference here is quality diversity over pure novelty, because an exploding or fragmented organism can be very novel. A simpler control is four independent small populations, with occasional migration and different mutation scales. For example, compare four populations of eight against one population of 32, migrating one policy every ten generations. Keep migration sparse enough that one lineage does not immediately take over all islands. These numerical settings are our proposed baseline, not a result from the cited work. A restart/immigrant control could instead reserve two of 16 candidate slots for fresh or previously diverse policies while preserving the champion.

**Antithetic ES learns from both sides of a perturbation.** OpenAI-ES estimates a search direction using population evaluations of perturbed parameters; mirrored perturbations and fitness ranking are useful components of that approach. It is compatible with black-box rollout fitness and avoids full covariance storage. [Salimans et al., Evolution Strategies as a Scalable Alternative to Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.03864).

In our setting, evaluate `w + sigma * D * epsilon` and `w - sigma * D * epsilon` on identical cases, and combine their results into an update. A 32-candidate batch supplies 16 paired directions, which may be insufficient to estimate a useful direction in thousands of dimensions. Test before assuming the combination is more informative than selection. Common cases reduce intentional case variation; they cannot guarantee cancellation of GPU execution-order noise. This is a reasonable lightweight baseline, especially for local refinement, but offers no diversity preservation by itself.

**Sensitivity-aware mutations may preserve learned behavior.** Safe Mutation through Gradients scales perturbations using the sensitivity of network outputs to parameters. The gradients are through the controller, not necessarily through the environment's fitness. It was designed to reduce destructive changes in deep or recurrent neuroevolution. [Lehman et al., Safe Mutations for Deep and Recurrent Neural Networks through Output Gradients](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.06563).

This suggests a feasible project adaptation: record representative neural inputs during real rollouts, reproduce the deployed output transforms in a differentiable reference, and estimate output sensitivity there. We already have a PyTorch policy reference, but its raw outputs alone are not the entire GPU behavioral mapping. Include the growth/chemical squashing and recurrent update semantics before using this metric. Short recurrent unrolls would give more information than isolated states. Small local output changes still need not remain small after 2,000 coupled steps.

A cheaper comparison is block mutation: perturb growth, chemistry, color, or trunk parameters separately for some children, while retaining full-network mutations. This can test whether color refinement is unnecessarily disturbing shape control. It can also miss coordinated changes across blocks, so it should not become the sole proposal operator.

**NCA pooling trains a distribution of states, not a collection of genomes.** Growing Neural Cellular Automata trains with backpropagation through unrolled updates. Its sample pool stores simulation states; batches are drawn from the pool, the highest-loss sampled state is replaced with a fresh seed, and resulting states are written back. The article's example uses 1,024 pooled states and batches of 32, with randomized rollout lengths of 64–96 updates. A further experiment damages states to teach regeneration. These are parameters of that grid NCA experiment, not suggested MPM defaults. [Mordvintsev et al., Growing Neural Cellular Automata](https://distill.pub/2020/growing-ca/).

The transferable idea is to train a rule that also improves partially developed or mature organisms. It could help our dynamics learn stopping, persistence, and repair. It does not itself create a route across a weight-space fitness valley, and it can reinforce the current pattern if the pool contains only that pattern.

For this project, state pooling requires engineering before optimizer changes:

- A resumable state must include geometry/rest state, positions, velocity, deformation and affine state, growth-cycle state, neural/private and cell-owned chemical state, persistent chemical fields and buffer parity, relevant grid history, active counts, and simulation time. Restore derived scratch state by a documented deterministic rebuild where possible. The existing `RolloutSnapshot` contains scoring data and positions; it is not a complete continuation checkpoint. [Snapshot type](trainer/rollout_snapshot.py), [state audit](DETERMINISM_INVESTIGATION.md).
- A capacity-terminal state currently stops immediately, so simply putting it in a pool produces no meaningful continuation. Implement a defined maintenance mode, adequate capacity, or another deliberate continuation rule. Freezing growth changes the task and must be reported. Do not assume every overshot organism can recover under the available material controls.
- Pool states generated by another network are off-policy developmental states. Store provenance and age, favor recent compatible states, and test whether the current policy can interpret their chemical/private memory. Updating the pool after each candidate would unfairly give candidates different objectives: freeze one common pool batch for each comparison.
- Maintain a substantial fresh-seed component and fresh-seed validation. Otherwise a policy could learn only to finish another policy's organism. Damage operators must preserve a meaningful mechanical state; deleting arbitrary mesh data is not a valid analogue of erasing image pixels.

My proposed pilot begins with 32–64 complete states and two evaluation cases per candidate: one fresh seed and one shared pooled state, initially using shorter continuations of roughly 100–300 macro steps. Build the pool from different developmental ages, not just final shapes. Compare against a fresh-only control with the same measured simulation cost. Start with continuation and mild supported perturbations; add damage only after restoration and mechanics invariants pass. Pooling can reduce repeated early-development work, but GPU state transfer and restoration may offset that saving.

**Curricula and selection over separate cases can make detail matter.** Our loss already includes multiscale coverage/spill, boundary, overlap, and color. Adding a generic coarse-to-fine objective is therefore not starting from a single-scale baseline. A targeted curriculum could first seek silhouette coverage, then increase detail and RGB pressure, and finally test persistence. Keep a fixed final evaluation objective throughout; losses at different curriculum stages cannot be compared directly.

Case-based selection is another option. Epsilon-lexicase filters candidates through randomly ordered cases with a tolerance, preserving specialists that average loss can discard. Its primary evidence includes regression tasks, not this simulator. [La Cava et al., Epsilon-Lexicase Selection for Regression](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.13266).

Our adaptation could treat normalized head, limb, tail, and body-region errors as cases, alongside seed/density conditions. This is useful only if regional measures are stable under pose alignment and meaningful for the target. Restricting attention to individual regions can preserve incomplete specialists indefinitely. A fixed validated whole-shape champion remains necessary. Similarly, a Pareto archive of silhouette, color, and persistence tradeoffs is worth considering if a single weighted sum repeatedly hides silhouette regressions; it is a separate experiment from morphology-based MAP-Elites.

**Differentiable simulation would change the training economics, but requires a substantial new path.** DiffTaichi demonstrates gradient-based optimization through physical simulators using differentiable programming. That supports investigating gradient-based morphogenesis, not assuming this WGSL pipeline can already backpropagate. [Hu et al., DiffTaichi](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.00935).

Our live inference and mechanics run outside PyTorch autograd. Refinement allocation, capacity termination, CPU raster scoring, and discrete pose search add discontinuities or missing derivatives. A credible first prototype would fix topology over a short horizon, use differentiable mechanics and occupancy loss, and optimize a restricted controller. It would need forward-parity and gradient checks before judging learning quality. Success on that surrogate would still require evaluation in the actual growth simulator. A hybrid could evolve global structure and locally refine a differentiable surrogate, but rollout mismatch could erase those gains.

I would give vanilla crossover, particle swarm, ordinary high-dimensional Bayesian optimization, and a wholesale RL replacement lower priority. In this network, hidden-unit permutation symmetry makes naive weight crossover difficult to interpret, and none of those substitutions automatically solves noisy fitness or loss of developmental stepping stones. This is a project prioritization judgment, not evidence that those algorithm families cannot work.

**A benchmark that can distinguish improvement from extra compute.** The following protocol is proposed; it has not been executed.

| Stage | Experiment | Decision it enables |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Repeat fixed weights under current code; log loss components, stop reason/time, descriptor variation | Set noise-aware promotion rules and mutation calibration |
| 1 | Existing GA; mutation ladder; ladder + restarts; separable CMA; LM-MA-ES | Does distribution adaptation beat inexpensive controls? |
| 2 | Gaussian MAP-Elites; single adaptive search; archive + adaptive emitters | Does preserving behavioral diversity improve the best result? |
| 3 | Winning optimizer with fresh-only versus mixed state-pool evaluation | Does continuation training improve persistence without losing growth? |
| 4 | Block/sensitivity mutation, detailed curriculum, or differentiable prototype | Address the failure mode that remains after the earlier comparisons |

Use at least five independent training RNG seeds in an initial screen, then expand promising comparisons to ten. Include `circle`, `legs`, and `lizard-256` to distinguish easy convergence, branching, and fine detail. Run from-scratch search and warm-start refinement as separate tracks. Share initial populations or reference policies where compatible, and share case schedules. Start with one fixed architecture and chemical configuration; test recurrent memory later as a separate factor.

Use two budget tiers, for example **800 and 3,200 total training rollout calls per run**, counting reevaluations and restarts. The first matches the nominal 16 × 50 default; the larger tier allows slower adaptation to emerge. Also compare progress against actual wall time and total simulated macro steps, since capacity and low-growth stops give candidates very different costs. A 32-member method gets half as many generations as a 16-member method at equal rollout budget. Pool experiments need a matched wall-time view because continuation calls are shorter.

Before tuning, reserve fixed validation cases and a separate final test set. With `none`, validation repetitions measure execution robustness, not initial-condition generalization. To assess developmental robustness, explicitly define perturbations, strengths, densities, and seeds, and reserve unseen combinations. Use the same case suites for every algorithm. Do not vary density, fitness alignment, target resolution, or stopping law between optimizer arms.

For a modest initial promotion policy, evaluate candidate champions and the incumbent on four matched validation cases/repeats, increasing to eight when the difference is inconclusive. Treat this as an engineering starting point; sequential winner selection creates bias, so the final untouched test set is still required. Define a practically meaningful improvement threshold after measuring noise. Never promote a record solely because it beats an old single-rollout minimum.

Record validated loss versus wall time and rollout budget; silhouette and RGB separately; missing/spill/overlap; stop reason and growth timing; late-state drift; unique behavior count and archive coverage; mutation success by scale; lineage survival; and optimizer overhead. Report medians, spread, and paired differences across independent training runs. Select the final policy using validation, then evaluate it once through the predeclared test protocol. For the stability track, add an extended rollout or defined maintenance evaluation; ordinary capacity-stop fitness cannot demonstrate persistence.

**Implementation path.** Separate candidate generation, evaluation, and selection behind an `ask / evaluate / tell` interface. Keep `worker_rollout` and the process pool as the evaluator, returning structured per-case losses, compact descriptors, and diagnostics. Avoid returning full rasters for every candidate when compact measurements suffice. The current routine couples sorting, elitism, reproduction, and winner extraction; a new optimizer must not assume its mean or its first proposed sample is the best evaluated policy.

Store both the best validated policy and complete optimizer continuation state: distribution mean/scales/directions, population or archive, RNG states, evaluation-case schedule, validation estimates, counters, and schema/config/source identity. A weight-only warm start is not an optimizer resume. Preserve the existing GA as a regression and scientific baseline. Check candidate/score association, minimization sign, coordinate transforms, resume behavior, and invalid-fitness handling before expensive trials.

For implementation candidates, official `pycma` provides CMA-ES, and pyribs provides archives, emitters, and scalable evolution strategies. Pin and record the selected versions after a compatibility check; no dependencies were installed for this survey. [pycma documentation](https://cma-es.github.io/apidocs-pycma/), [pyribs optimizer API](https://docs.pyribs.org/en/latest/api/ribs.emitters.opt.html).

The recommended first deliverable is this evaluator/optimizer separation, reliable champion validation, and separable CMA-ES with restart support. The next is a small morphology archive with explicit descriptor diagnostics. Those steps directly test whether the current stagnation comes from proposal quality or discarded alternatives, while leaving a clear route to limited-memory quality diversity and later NCA continuation training.
